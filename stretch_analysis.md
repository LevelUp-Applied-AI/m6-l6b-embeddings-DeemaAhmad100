I organized 200 GloVe words into five intentionally broad semantic categories (countries, sports, finance, technology, emotions) to test whether embeddings separate entities, activities, domains, and affective vocabulary, then used t-SNE for both word and document plots because this stretch task focuses on local neighborhood structure and cluster interpretability. In the word plot, technology forms a clear upper-right cluster around terms like internet/ai, finance forms a lower-right cluster around bank/stock, sports is mostly isolated on the left around football/basketball, countries concentrate in the lower-center band around france/japan, and emotions occupy an upper-center region around happy/angry/emotion; these separations suggest the GloVe space preserves strong domain-specific neighborhoods, while a few cross-category outliers near boundaries indicate words with mixed usage contexts. In the BBC DistilBERT document plot (20 articles, 4 per category), sport is the most clearly separated group in the lower region, entertainment sits to the left-mid area, and tech/business/politics are closer on the right side with partial overlap, which is expected because policy, markets, and technology reporting often share vocabulary about regulation, companies, and economic impact; notably, at least one business article appears nearer to politics/business boundary, showing topic blending rather than a strict single-cluster assignment.
